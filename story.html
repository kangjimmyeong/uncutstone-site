Our Story

A classroom puzzle, carried softly into the palm of your hand

01 · Classroom

The puzzle began in a classroom

Seven Puzzle began as a teacher's tool: seven numbered wooden blocks, made for children to hold, turn, and see space through their hands.

In Korea's public-school classrooms and gifted-education programs, about a thousand students picked up those pieces. Small hands traced the wood grain, tried again, and heard the quiet click of a fit.

03 · The game

Now it becomes a game

Read the small blueprint cube floating above. Turn a piece in your mind, then in your hand. Press and hold, and it settles with a soft snap and a gentle pulse.

The 200 stages climb from one piece to the full seven-piece cube. Later stages veil parts of the blueprint, so you hold more of the shape in your head. If you choose, the Spatial Intelligence Report shows your percentile among players worldwide.

There are no timers, no lives, no failure screens, and no forced ads. It works offline, supports 12 languages, and has dark mode. After every stage, one quiet line appears — 200 stages, 200 different lines, including “Your effort shines quietly.”

Education is slow, but certain — and sometimes it stays beside you as a quiet game.
